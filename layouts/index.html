Section 01

The Core Manifesto

Software should adapt to people, not force people to adapt to software.

Modern software provides extraordinary capabilities, yet many applications make ordinary work dependent on remote services, vendor accounts, opaque decisions, and business models that can change without the user's consent. These arrangements can be convenient, but convenience becomes dependence when a person cannot continue working, retrieve their records, or leave a provider without unacceptable loss.

Sovereign Software is a deliberate alternative: software designed so that ultimate authority remains with the person or organization using it, not with a vendor, an infrastructure operator, or an unaccountable algorithm. It need not reject networks, commercial software, artificial intelligence, or complex engineering. It rejects unnecessary surrender of control.

Section 03

Architecture in Service of the Principles

No single architecture defines Sovereign Software, because the right design depends on the domain. A useful pattern is to distinguish three conceptual responsibilities. They are not a requirement for three separate applications: an integrated application can embody all three, and a collection of specialized applications can fail them.

Execution

Carrying out tasks, transitions, calculations, and other active workflows.

Preservation

Retaining records, context, metadata, and recovery information so they remain intelligible beyond the current application.

Exchange

Transferring information between tools through documented formats, protocols, APIs, or import and export mechanisms.

Interoperability is a means of preserving sovereignty and longevity, not an independent ideological demand for small programs. Modularity and bounded responsibilities remain sound engineering practice, but software does not become non-sovereign simply because it offers many features. Architecture should make information portable, important behavior understandable, and essential functions recoverable.

Section 04

AI Without Artificial Dependency

AI can accelerate development and make sophisticated tools more accessible, but it also introduces probabilistic outputs, opaque service dependencies, and actions that can exceed the user's intent. Sovereign Software uses AI only under boundaries that preserve human authority:

  • ✓ Existing records remain accessible if a model or provider becomes unavailable.
  • ✓ Core workflows that do not inherently require AI remain usable without it.
  • ✓ Consequential changes proposed by AI are validated and authorized according to their risk.
  • ✓ Network calls, privacy implications, and costs are disclosed.
  • ✓ AI outputs the user keeps can be preserved in independently usable formats.
  • ✓ Important decisions and modifications can be inspected, explained, and corrected.

AI may extend human ability. It must not become a condition for retaining human work or authority.

Section 05

Synchronization Without Surrender

Synchronizing devices is useful, but it must not quietly transfer custody or conceal loss. When disconnected devices edit the same information, an automatic merge can reconcile data structures while still misunderstanding human intent.

  • Conflict rules: Documented, with recoverable history where feasible, and with conflicts that cannot be resolved safely exposed for a decision.
  • Transport versus ownership: Users know where data is stored, who can read it, how it is encrypted, and what happens when the service disappears. Where practical, the mechanism is replaceable.
  • Backups: Replicated data is not a tested backup, because mistakes and corruption synchronize as readily as useful changes.

Synchronization should connect a person's devices, not make their work depend on a provider's continued existence.

Section 07

What Sovereign Software Refuses to Become

✕ Captive Data: Records that cannot be meaningfully retrieved, interpreted, or migrated.
✕ Revocable Access to One's Own Work: Essential information or results made inaccessible by an arbitrary account or subscription decision.
✕ Irrecoverable Dependence: Information that becomes useless when one vendor, binary, or runtime disappears.
✕ Opaque Consequential Automation: Significant actions performed without appropriate visibility, authorization, or recourse.
✕ Silent Loss Through Synchronization: Conflicting or corrupted states overwritten without practical recovery.
✕ Concealed Dependencies and Surveillance: Mandatory external calls or data collection that undermine informed user choice.

It does not reject sophisticated applications, commercial development, optional cloud services, AI, or feature-rich designs merely for being sophisticated, commercial, connected, intelligent, or comprehensive. The issue is the resulting distribution of authority and the durability of user freedom.

The ability to build or monetize a feature is not sufficient reason to make users dependent on it.

Section 08

Personal Software in the Age of AI

Modern tools and AI-assisted workflows can lower some of the barriers that favored mass-market software, making tailored applications more feasible for individuals, research groups, and smaller communities. But lower development cost does not guarantee lower lifetime cost: security, migration, maintenance, documentation, accessibility, and data recovery still need sustained attention, and AI-generated code can be faster to create without being easier to trust or preserve.

Software does not need a mass market to justify its existence. It does need to justify the dependencies it imposes on its users.

Section 09

The Enduring Commitment

Sovereign Software is not defined by a database engine, a local-first framework, a language, a licensing label, or a synchronization protocol. Its enduring commitments are simpler: authority stays with the user; information survives the tools that produced it; unnecessary external dependence is resisted; and important actions remain transparent and governable.

When the software, vendor, network, or fashionable technology changes, does the person still retain meaningful control over their work?
