Every foundational principle in the Sovereign Software Manifesto has substantial intellectual and technical precedents. We document those precedents in detail for two reasons:

1. **Intellectual honesty:** A manifesto that writes as if user authority, local execution, format longevity, and algorithmic accountability were discovered yesterday misleads its readers. Presenting Sovereign Software as a **synthesis and evaluation approach** rather than an invention from nothing makes its actual claims clearer and more credible.
2. **Clarifying our motive and where we stand:** Each earlier movement solved a crucial part of the problem while leaving other failure modes open. Free software protects legal code freedom, yet a user's records can still be trapped inside an open-source cloud service. Local-first software keeps replicas on the device, yet a proprietary client or closed sync relay can still revoke operational continuity. Digital preservation defines archival durability, yet is normally applied by institutions after software is retired rather than used to evaluate everyday tools while people depend on them.

Our motive in building the Sovereign Software framework is to **bridge those blind spots**: bringing four architecture-neutral principles (*Sovereignty*, *Longevity*, *Locality*, and *Transparency and Control*) together with five non-compensatory eligibility requirements (`E1–E5`), seven verification procedures (`T1–T7`), and domain-specific preservation profiles (`Section 1 of the methodology`) so that practical user authority can be tested rather than assumed from a label.

## Where the four principles come from

| Principle | Primary intellectual precedents | What Sovereign Software synthesizes and tests |
|---|---|---|
| **I. Sovereignty**<br>*Authority belongs to the user* | [Free Software (GNU)](/lineage/free-software/), [IndieWeb](/lineage/indieweb/), [Interoperability & Exit](/lineage/interoperability-and-exit/), [MyData](/lineage/mydata/), [Solid](/lineage/solid/), [Local-first Software](/lineage/local-first/) | Evaluates practical authority across four dimensions (information, execution, governance, and continuity) under a provider-withdrawal scenario (`E1`, `E2`, `E4`, `T1`), rather than relying on license category or architectural labels alone. |
| **II. Longevity**<br>*Information outlives software* | [Digital Preservation (LoC & OAIS)](/lineage/digital-preservation/), [Local-first Software](/lineage/local-first/), [Permacomputing](/lineage/permacomputing/) | Applies archival sustainability criteria (`ISO 14721:2025` representation information and Library of Congress format factors) prospectively to live software via clean-machine recovery tests (`E3`, `T2`) and domain preservation profiles. |
| **III. Locality**<br>*Independence is designed in* | [Free Software (SaaSS critique)](/lineage/free-software/), [IndieWeb](/lineage/indieweb/), [Local-first Software](/lineage/local-first/), [Permacomputing](/lineage/permacomputing/) | Evaluates offline capability alongside **dependency proportionality and replaceability** (`T3`): inherent network functions are permitted when external relays or servers can be substituted without vendor permission. |
| **IV. Transparency and Control**<br>*Consequential behavior is governable* | [Free Software](/lineage/free-software/), [MyData](/lineage/mydata/), [Design Justice](/lineage/design-justice/), [NIST AI RMF 1.0](/lineage/ai-governance/) | Evaluates inspectability, proportionate authorization, rollback/recourse for consequential actions (`E5`, `T4`), and treats external AI services as withdrawable operational dependencies. |

## Comparative overview of related movements (chronological order)

The comparisons below and in each individual dossier represent qualitative analyses of conceptual overlap, ordered from the earliest historical starting point to the most recent.

| Period & Movement | Overlap | Core thesis | Where Sovereign Software stands & why it diverges |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1983–Present**<br>**[Free Software (GNU)](/lineage/free-software/)** | Very high | Users must possess four legal freedoms to run, study, modify, and redistribute software, and reject services that substitute for local execution (SaaSS). | We evaluate **practical operational authority** over information and workflows rather than making open-source licensing an absolute gate, while explicitly testing where a closed binary threatens long-term functional continuity (`E4`, `T6`). |
| **1995–2025**<br>**[Digital Preservation (LoC & OAIS)](/lineage/digital-preservation/)** | High | Preserve digital objects junto with the representation information and context required for a future community to understand them (`ISO 14721:2025`). | Archival frameworks are typically applied by repositories after records are created. We translate OAIS and Library of Congress format criteria into prospective engineering tests (`T2`, `E3`) for everyday software. |
| **2010–Present**<br>**[IndieWeb](/lineage/indieweb/)** | High | Own your online identity and content on a personal domain, syndicating outward (POSSE) without surrendering primary custody to platforms. | We adopt IndieWeb's custody logic (using external networks without surrendering primary ownership) and generalize it beyond web publishing to engineering, financial, scientific, and desktop/server software. |
| **2012–Present**<br>**[Interoperability & Exit (EFF / Right to Repair)](/lineage/interoperability-and-exit/)** | High | Competitive compatibility and low switching costs discipline monopolies and protect user privacy alongside data protection rules. | We translate policy arguments for interoperability and Right to Repair into falsifiable product-level exit and migration tests (`E2`, `T1`, `T6`). |
| **2014–Present**<br>**[MyData](/lineage/mydata/)** | High | Shift personal data governance from formal legal consent to actionable human agency, portability, and interoperable data control. | MyData focuses primarily on personal data held by external organizations. Sovereign Software requires preservation of **functional capability and workflow continuity**, not only raw data downloads. |
| **2015–Present**<br>**[Solid Project](/lineage/solid/)** | High | Decouple applications from personal data stores (Pods) using linked-data standards and granular access control. | Solid is a concrete architectural implementation of our separation between execution, preservation, and exchange. We treat Pod architectures as candidates that must still pass empirical exit, recovery, and provider-withdrawal tests. |
| **2015–Present**<br>**[Design Justice](/lineage/design-justice/)** | Moderate | Center communities disproportionately harmed by design decisions and prioritize collective accountability over designer intent. | Design Justice exposes a blind spot in purely individualistic software philosophies: multi-user governance conflicts. This directly motivated our **perspective roles** and shared governance rules in the methodology. |
| **2019–Present**<br>**[Local-first Software](/lineage/local-first/)** | Very high | Keep primary data on local devices while enabling multi-device collaboration via conflict-free synchronization (such as CRDTs). | Our closest technical predecessor. We diverge by remaining architecture-neutral (evaluating managed and client-server tools where exit and recovery are real) and by adding explicit tests for consequential actions (`E5`) and non-compensatory eligibility failures. |
| **2020–Present**<br>**[Permacomputing](/lineage/permacomputing/)** | High | Design computing systems for long-term resilience, low resource consumption, hardware longevity, and minimal fragile dependencies. | We share Permacomputing's hostility to fragile dependency chains and planned obsolescence, while focusing specifically on verifiable user authority and work preservation in mainstream personal and professional tools. |
| **2021–Present**<br>**[Human-Centered AI Governance (NIST)](/lineage/ai-governance/)** | Moderate–high | Manage AI risk through validity, reliability, transparency, explainability, privacy, and human oversight (`NIST AI 100-1`). | Beyond output reliability and fairness, we evaluate AI features as **withdrawable external dependencies**: users must retain custody of their underlying records and local workflows if an AI provider changes terms or shuts down. |

## Research roadmap

What we have not found in our initial prior-art review is a single framework that combines architecture-neutral principles, non-compensatory minimum eligibility requirements (`E1–E5`), domain-specific preservation profiles, and a deterministic, evidence-led evaluation procedure across both local and networked software. Because that observation comes from a bounded literature review rather than an exhaustive survey of all software quality standards, we treat it as a **research hypothesis** to be tested through three planned studies:

1. **A formal comparative position paper:** Defining practical software sovereignty across information, execution, governance, and continuity authority; contrasting the ten traditions documented here; and analyzing the two hardest theoretical tensions (when operational independence can or cannot substitute for software license freedom, and how individual exit interacts with legitimate collective governance).
2. **An inter-rater reproducibility study:** Applying the [Two-Assessor Protocol](/methodology/#sufficiency-decision-and-two-assessor-protocol) and domain preservation profiles (`Section 1 of the methodology`) across independent evaluators to measure where observations, scope assumptions, and threshold judgments converge or diverge.
3. **A cross-architecture empirical evaluation:** Running the full six-stage laboratory protocol (`T1–T7`) across contrasting categories of software (open-source vs. proprietary, local desktop vs. managed service, plain-text vs. binary repository) to demonstrate that the framework identifies real custody and recovery failures that single-axis labels miss.
